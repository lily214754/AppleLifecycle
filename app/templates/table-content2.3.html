Orchard Management Strategies for Mature Apple Trees

Objective Suggested Operations for Orchard Management
- Increase fruit size and quality
- Increase return bloom
- Reduce incident of biennial bearing 		Thinning:
Remove buds, flowers, or fruits manually, mechanically, or chemically, depending on the method. This operation generally occurs once per season, with supplementary hand thinning later if the effect of initial thinning on fruit size or return bloom is not sufficient \cite{stoverMethodAssessingRelationship2001};
Control 4-6 fruits per square cm of trunk cross-sectional area (TCSA), varying by variety \cite{robinsonCropLoadManagement2008}.
- Prevent canopy of tree out of allotted space
- Control canopy density to better light interception and spraying penetration 		Pruning & Training:
Remove shoots or limbs to maintain canopy within space during the dormant and summer seasons annually. See different pruning plans at \cite{ferreeApplesBotanyProduction2003}. In larger orchards, pruning may be scheduled every second or third year to manage costs \cite{ferreeApplesBotanyProduction2003}.
- Provide appropriate nutrition to support tree growth and increase fruit quality Fertilization:
Apply once in the spring and once in the autumn after fruiting \cite{howFertilizeApple2023}; Integrated Orchard Management Guide for Commercial Apples in the Southeast, 2023 \cite{2023IntegratedOrchard2023}.
- Modify growth and characteristic of fruit Plant growth regulator:
Apply plant growth regulator to adjust the growth and characteristics of tree and fruit as necessary. For practical recommendation tailored to different developmental stages, refer to \cite{2023IntegratedOrchardManagement2023}.
- Optimal usage of water resources to supply adequate water, maintain suitable soil moisture level to avoid tree stress Irrigation schedule:
Follow the guidelines for mature trees, as in Table \ref{ATL_tab04}
Mature trees may require more frequent irrigation \cite{dzikitiEstimatingWaterRequirements2018}.

Follow the guidelines for mature trees, as in Table \ref{ATL_tab04}

- Protect against damage from birds and extreme weather condition 		Light Management:
