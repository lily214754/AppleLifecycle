Cell division Begins shortly after flowering and typically ceases about 4-6 weeks after bloom \cite{ferreeApplesBotanyProduction2003} and vary significantly among different cultivars \cite{patriciadenneFruitDevelopmentTree1963}. Cell numbers are established within the first 35-50 days after full blossom \cite{denneGrowthAppleFruitlets1960} Fruits experience exponential growth in fruit size and period for cell number increase \cite{ferreeApplesBotanyProduction2003}.

Cell enlargement /expansion The primary phase of cell expansion typically begins as the rate of cell division slows down and eventually stops, peaking between 40 to 60 days after anthesis. This expansion phase continues at a slower rate for the remainder of the growth period until harvest \cite{bainPhysiologyGrowthApple1951, ferreeApplesBotanyProduction2003, janssenGlobalGeneExpression2008, patriciadenneFruitDevelopmentTree1963, wunscheRelationshipLeafArea2000} Fruits experience linear growth in size through cell enlargement \cite{ferreeApplesBotanyProduction2003}, which leads to an increase in cell volume and intercellular spaces, while the cell packing density decreases \cite{janssenGlobalGeneExpression2008}
